# What is Human Error in Cybersecurity 2026
> **Quick answer:** Human error in cybersecurity refers to the unintentional actions or decisions made by individuals that can compromise the security of an organization's digital assets. This can include mistakes such as using weak passwords, clicking on phishing links, or failing to update software.

As a cybersecurity professional, you're likely aware of the significant threat that human error poses to your organization's digital security. With the increasing reliance on digital technologies, the risk of human error has become a major concern, and it's essential to understand the causes and consequences of such errors. ## What are the causes of human error in cybersecurity?
**Human error is often caused by a combination of factors, including lack of training, inadequate procedures, and insufficient resources**. Human error can occur due to various reasons, such as a lack of awareness about cybersecurity best practices, inadequate training, or insufficient resources. For instance, a study by IBM found that 60% of organizations that experienced a data breach in 2020 attributed the breach to human error.

## How does human error impact cybersecurity?
**Human error can have severe consequences, including data breaches, financial losses, and reputational damage**. The impact of human error on cybersecurity can be significant, with the average cost of a data breach reaching $3.86 million in 2020, according to a report by IBM. Furthermore, human error can also lead to compliance issues, as organizations may fail to meet regulatory requirements due to mistakes made by employees.

## What are the most common types of human error in cybersecurity?
**The most common types of human error include phishing, password misuse, and inadequate software updates**. Phishing attacks, for example, are a common type of human error, where employees may click on malicious links or provide sensitive information to attackers. A study by Verizon found that 30% of data breaches in 2020 involved phishing attacks.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Phishing | Clicking on malicious links or providing sensitive information to attackers | An employee receives an email that appears to be from a legitimate source, but is actually a phishing attack |
| Password Misuse | Using weak passwords or failing to update passwords regularly | An employee uses a weak password, such as "password123", which can be easily guessed by attackers |
| Inadequate Software Updates | Failing to update software or plugins, leaving them vulnerable to attacks | An employee fails to update a plugin, which is then exploited by attackers to gain access to the organization's network |

**Organizations can mitigate human error by implementing robust training programs, conducting regular security audits, and using AI-powered tools**.
