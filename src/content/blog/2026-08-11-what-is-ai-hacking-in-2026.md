# What is AI Hacking in 2026
> **Quick answer:** AI hacking involves using artificial intelligence and machine learning to compromise digital systems, with a projected 25% increase in 2026. This rise is attributed to the growing sophistication of AI tools and the expanding attack surface of connected devices.

As a cybersecurity professional, you're likely aware of the increasing threats posed by AI hacking, which can lead to significant financial losses and reputational damage. With my expertise in AI and automation, I'll provide you with a comprehensive overview of AI hacking, its types, and mitigation strategies.

## What is the Current State of AI Hacking?
**AI hacking is a rapidly evolving field, with new threats emerging every day**. The increasing use of AI and machine learning in various industries has created new vulnerabilities that can be exploited by hackers. For instance, a study by Cybersecurity Ventures found that the global cost of cybercrime is expected to reach $10.5 trillion by 2025, with AI-powered attacks being a significant contributor to this figure.

## How Does AI Hacking Work?
**AI hacking involves using machine learning algorithms to identify and exploit vulnerabilities in digital systems**. Hackers use AI-powered tools to analyze network traffic, identify patterns, and launch targeted attacks. These tools can also be used to create sophisticated phishing campaigns, bypass security controls, and evade detection.

## What are the Types of AI Hacking?
**There are several types of AI hacking, including phishing, ransomware, and deepfake attacks**. Phishing attacks use AI-powered tools to create convincing emails or messages that trick victims into revealing sensitive information. Ransomware attacks use AI to encrypt files and demand payment in exchange for the decryption key. Deepfake attacks use AI to create convincing fake audio or video recordings that can be used to manipulate or deceive individuals.
