When you decide to sell your business, you're likely aware of the financial and operational implications of such a transaction. However, it's equally as important to consider the intellectual property component of your business, particularly your business name. Do you still maintain ownership of your business name after the sale? The answer to this question lies in understanding the nuances of business ownership and the rights associated with your brand identity.

The FT recently published an article titled "Business owners struggle to keep control of their brand names as companies are bought and sold". According to this report, an increasing number of business sellers are finding themselves in disputes with their new owners over the rights to their business names. This situation has led to questions about the nature of business ownership and what happens to your intellectual property when you sell your company.

So, what happens to your business name after the sale? 

Typically, when you sell your business, you transfer the ownership of all assets, including your business name, to the new owner. The business name is usually a registered trademark or a service mark, which are types of intellectual property that can be transferred as part of the sale. In many cases, the new owner is granted the exclusive right to use the business name and its associated intellectual property.

However, there are scenarios where you may not be required to transfer the ownership of your business name. For instance, if you retain a licensing agreement, you may still have the right to use your business name and its associated intellectual property, albeit with certain restrictions.

Another option is negotiating a royalty agreement with the new owner. This arrangement allows you to retain some level of ownership or control over your business name and intellectual property, usually in exchange for a payment or percentage of sales.

But what if there's been no transfer of ownership, and you're still using the business name? Do you still maintain ownership?

The answer depends on the specific circumstances surrounding the sale. For instance, if your business operates as a sole proprietorship or a partnership, you may not be able to formally transfer ownership of the business name. In these cases, the new owner may not have a clear right to use the business name.

However, if your business operates as a limited liability company (LLC) or a corporation, the transfer of ownership is typically more formalized, and the new owner typically assumes all rights associated with the business name.

Understanding these nuances is crucial to protecting your brand identity and maintaining control over your business name.

What rights do you, as a business seller, have in this situation?

To protect your rights and maintain control over your business name, consider the following:

- Negotiate a licensing agreement with the new owner. 
- Retain a royalty agreement with the new owner. 
- Consider setting up a holding company to retain ownership of the business name.
- Research and understand your rights as a sole proprietor, partnership, LLC, or corporation.
- Be prepared to litigate in case of disputes with the new owner.

Your business name and brand identity are significant components of your intellectual property. Do you still maintain ownership of your business name after the sale? The answer lies in the specific circumstances surrounding the sale and your business's structure.

While the new owner typically assumes all rights associated with the business name, there are scenarios where you may retain some level of ownership or control. Protecting your brand identity requires understanding the nuances of business ownership and negotiating agreements with your new owner.

As a business seller, it's essential to be aware of your rights and take steps to maintain control over your business name. This involves researching the specific circumstances surrounding the sale, your business's structure, and negotiating agreements with your new owner.

Ultimately, protecting your brand identity requires ongoing vigilance. Be proactive about understanding your rights and taking steps to safeguard your business name and intellectual property.
