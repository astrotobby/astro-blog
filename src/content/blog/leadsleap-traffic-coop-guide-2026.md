## How the Traffic Co-op works

LeadsLeap gives members a unique Co-op URL in the format `https://lltrco.com/?r=username`. Members promote that link through other traffic networks and relevant external channels. LeadsLeap's official introduction says a visitor must surf the Co-op page for at least five seconds before the visit counts. It describes the program as a reciprocal traffic network: members send visitors to the Co-op and receive traffic back through the network.

A visit is not the same thing as a qualified visit. A page load, an accidental click or an automated hit is not a reliable basis for estimating income. The visitor needs to choose to visit and actually use the page as the program requires.

Official background: [LeadsLeap's Traffic Co-op introduction (July 22, 2022)](https://leadsleap.com/blog/new-introducing-leadsleap-traffic-coop/) and [LeadsLeap's Traffic Co-op progress update (July 26, 2022)](https://leadsleap.com/blog/leadsleap-traffic-coop-progress-update/).

## What can earn credits and cash?

LeadsLeap's current member tutorial describes five income methods:

- **Affiliate commissions:** LeadsLeap says members can earn 25%–50% recurring commissions when referred members upgrade. The exact rate depends on membership status and the program's current terms.
- **Co-op earnings:** The tutorial says a visitor who surfs your Co-op link for at least five seconds can generate both credits and cash.
- **PPC earnings:** This is separate from Co-op earnings. LeadsLeap says an Ad Widget on your website can earn PPC income when a visitor voluntarily clicks an ad and then surfs the advertised site.
- **Credit encashment:** Credits can be used for advertising or converted into cash under the program's current encashment rules.
- **Daily Active Bonus:** The current tutorial says members must personally view at least ten ads in a day to qualify. Co-op/PPC visits do not count as personal ad views for this requirement.

Read the [official member tutorial](https://leadsleap.com/members/tutorial.php) for the current descriptions and conditions. The rules and rates shown inside a member account should be checked before making financial decisions.

## Treat advertised earning ranges carefully

LeadsLeap's Traffic Co-op introduction, published on July 22, 2022, stated a range of $0.10–$8 CPM, depending on traffic uniqueness, location and quality. Its July 26, 2022 progress update further explained that the amount for a thousand visitors could not be predicted in advance and was calculated after the platform reviewed traffic integrity.

Those are historical published figures, not a current rate card or a promise. They should not be used as a forecast for a new account. The volume and value of qualifying visits can vary, and a target such as $1 per day is not guaranteed.

## Cash, credits and withdrawals are different

Advertising credits are not the same as withdrawable cash. Credits may be used for advertising or encashed under the current program rules; Co-op cash and affiliate commissions are separate earning categories. LeadsLeap's current tutorial says its minimum withdrawal is $10 and explains that pending affiliate commissions are subject to a 60-day holding period.

Check the live [Income & Withdrawal](https://leadsleap.com/members/tutorial.php) guidance and your own dashboard for the latest thresholds, fees, payment methods and balance eligibility. Do not assume a credit balance is already payable cash.

## What automation is and is not allowed

LeadsLeap's [Terms of Use](https://leadsleap.com/terms.php) prohibit multiple accounts and automation—including bots, scripts, macros or software designed to simulate human activity—for surfing ads or interacting with its platform. The member tutorial also says accounts may be flagged for using bots or programs to automate or help with the surfing process.

I will not use auto-surf bots, browser macros, fake clicks, engagement farms or scripts that imitate human activity on LeadsLeap. Besides putting an account and its rewards at risk, manufactured visits do not create a genuine audience.

Automation can still support legitimate work outside the protected member activity: researching relevant subjects, drafting original educational content, scheduling posts on social accounts where the platform's rules permit it, measuring referral sources and summarizing genuine traffic results. Visitors should understand where a link leads and choose to visit.

## A sensible way to test the Co-op

1. Explain the visitor experience clearly before sharing your Co-op link.
2. Promote it only to audiences with a genuine interest in online marketing, website traffic or the tools being discussed.
3. Use source tags on links to the explanatory article so traffic sources can be compared without hiding the destination.
4. Record visits, qualifying visits, credits and cash separately.
5. Review the account's actual weekly earnings and withdrawal eligibility. Stop or change channels that produce irrelevant, low-quality or non-consensual traffic.

## Can it reliably make $1 a day?

There is not enough evidence to promise that a Free account will produce $1 every day. LeadsLeap itself says Co-op earnings depend on traffic quality and uniqueness, and its historical examples do not guarantee future results.

The useful test is to measure genuine visitors and actual cash credited over several weeks. Treat $1 per day as a target to evaluate, not as an expected or guaranteed result.
